# Grengin License FAQ

Short answers to the questions we get asked most. The LICENSE file
controls wherever this page and it appear to disagree.

## What license is Grengin under?

The Apache License 2.0, an OSI-approved open source license. Both public
repositories, grengin and grengin-api, carry the same license.

## What changed?

Earlier releases were published under the Grengin Sustainable Use License,
which reserved selling, hosting for third parties, and white-labeling to
Perter. Apache 2.0 drops all of those reservations. There is no revenue
threshold, no enterprise license, and no commercial license to buy.

## Do I need a commercial license?

No. There is no commercial license any more. Any organisation of any size
can use Grengin commercially, free of charge.

## Can I host or resell Grengin as a service?

Yes. You cannot market that service under the Grengin name or logo without
a written agreement with Perter, but describing it accurately, say "a
managed service built on Grengin", is fine. See TRADEMARKS.md.

## Can I fork Grengin?

Yes. A fork needs its own name and logo (TRADEMARKS.md) and has to keep the
LICENSE and NOTICE files, which Apache 2.0 requires.

## Can I embed Grengin in my own product?

Yes, including paid and proprietary products. Ship the LICENSE and NOTICE
files with the code.

## What do I have to do when I redistribute Grengin?

Include LICENSE, include NOTICE, keep the copyright notices, and say which
files you changed. That is the entire list. Section 4 of the license has
the exact wording.

## Does Grengin grant me any patents?

Yes. Section 3 grants you a patent license covering the contributions in
the software, and terminates it for anyone who sues over patents in it.
Nothing to sign.

## Do contributors have to sign a CLA?

No. Section 5 of the license already says that anything you submit for
inclusion comes in under the project's own license. Grengin is developed
in-house at Perter, so there is no contributor agreement to administer.

## What are older releases licensed under?

Whichever license they shipped with. Anything published before this change
stays under the Sustainable Use License; from this release onward it is
Apache 2.0. Upgrading is the simple path.
